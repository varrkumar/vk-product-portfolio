---
title: Redesigning how researchers share unfinished ideas
tagline: When the barrier isn't the UI — it's the social contract.
problem: >
  A sharing feature in a research tool had near-zero adoption despite correct
  discoverability and no user errors. The brief assumed UI confusion. The
  actual problem was different.
role: Lead product designer
team: 2 engineers, 1 researcher
timeline: 14 weeks (2024)
constraints:
  - Could not change the underlying data model — shared items are immutable
  - No A/B infrastructure at the time, so concept tests only
  - Engineering capacity was 1 sprint post-research
decisions:
  - options:
      - Redesign the sharing UI
      - Add a "draft" label to shared items
      - Reframe the action as a feedback request
    chosen: Reframe the action as a feedback request
    rationale: >
      UI redesign didn't address the root cause. Draft labelling helped but
      still positioned the researcher as the author of a finished thing.
      Feedback-request framing recast the social dynamic entirely.
    confidence: observed
outcome:
  summary: >
    Sharing events increased 3.1× in the first 30 days. Median share
    recipients rose from 1.0 to 2.4, suggesting researchers were sharing
    with networks, not just one safe contact.
  metrics:
    - 3.1× sharing events (day 1–30)
    - 2.4 median recipients (up from 1.0)
    - "[TODO: 90-day retention data]"
reflection: >
  I spent two weeks on UI fixes before questioning the brief. The lesson
  recurs: resist the pull of the solution space until the problem is actually
  defined.
order: 1
draft: false
